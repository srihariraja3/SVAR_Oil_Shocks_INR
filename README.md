# SVAR_Oil_Shocks_INR
Structural Oil Shocks and the Indian Rupee: An SVAR Decomposition (2000–2026) | Python | MySQL | Power BI

This paper applies the Kilian (2009) structural decomposition framework to examine whether crude oil price increases driven by geopolitical supply disruptions and precautionary demand generate statistically distinct spillovers into INR/USD exchange rate volatility compared to oil price increases driven by global aggregate demand expansions. Using a Cholesky-identified 4-variable SVAR (6) model estimated on monthly data from January 2000 to March 2026, global oil price movements are decomposed into three orthogonal structural shocks: crude oil supply shocks, aggregate demand shocks, and oil-specific precautionary demand shocks. Bootstrapped impulse response functions (500 replications, 24-month horizon) reveal that precautionary demand shocks generate a statistically significant, monotonically increasing Rupee depreciation accumulating to approximately 1.12% over 24 months — an effect that does not reverse within the estimation window. Aggregate demand shocks produce short-run Rupee appreciation, and supply shocks generate no statistically significant exchange rate response. Forecast error variance decomposition confirms a growing and unique contribution of precautionary shocks at longer horizons. These findings suggest that geopolitical oil price shocks produce persistent level shifts in the Rupee's equilibrium value through a distinct precautionary channel, with important implications for RBI intervention policy and corporate hedging strategy.
